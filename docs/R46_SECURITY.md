# R-46 Dot.Ehail: security, financial integrity and privacy review

Owner: security agent (branch `feat/r46-security`, decisions D-1400 to D-1418). Method: the `security-audit` skill in guidance mode (D-1400). Every finding below names a lower-trust principal, the boundary it crossed and the concrete result, was traced in code, and (medium and above) is fixed with a regression test in `tests/Feature/Security/`. Severity follows the skill's anchors: high = an explicit control fully defeated with real consequences (cross-tenant read/write, money taken or redirected); medium = a real boundary violation with limited reach; low = minor disclosure or effort for little gain. The POPIA record is `docs/R46_PRIVACY.md`.

## 2. Findings accepted or left to the owner (low, documented)

- Fee avoidance by removing the card or switching to cash before a late cancellation (`CancellationFees` drops the fee when the route is not card/business). Owner decision: record uncollected fees as receivable and include them in the payment-due block.
- Drop-off zone fees are not recalculated after a destination change (`PricingTripFareFinaliser` passes no `dropoffFeeCodes`). Pricing owner.
- No cap on how many scheduled rides a passenger holds (10 per minute by the request limiter). Dispatch owner.
- Incentives count every completed ride, including unpaid or waived ones, with no distinct-passenger rule (collusion farming). Money owner.
- An operator with rides.intervene can complete a legacy ride at any fare up to R999,999.99 (audited, charged automatically). Recommend a finance second approval above a threshold.
- Fare adjustments after settlement do not reconcile money's earning, ledger or payment (documented in R46_INTEGRATION section 9).
- The open board (`rides/available`) shows full pickup and drop-off labels of searching rides to eligible online drivers nationwide (no rider identity, no coordinates). Recommend suburb-level labels.
- The business guest phone is shown to the driver masked although the booking form says "shown to the driver". Wording or behaviour: business owner.
- A driver-created trip share is not disclosed to the passenger. Owner decision.
- Legacy `/fleets/{fleet}` admits any active fleet manager regardless of scope (names and statuses of applicants only).
- Trip PIN: 5 wrong tries lock for 10 minutes and the counter resets, so a determined guesser needs about a week; rides last minutes. Sound for the threat.
- Password-reset requests rely on the broker's 60-second per-email throttle; Fortify registers that route without a route limiter.
- `postcss-selector-parser` moderate advisory (build tooling only; fix requires a breaking downgrade).
- The service worker does not register inside the review browser pane ("unknown error when fetching the script", also with the CSP in report-only mode): an environment limit of the pane, not the policy.

## 3. Authorisation, mechanically (scope 1)

`tests/Feature/Security/RouteAuthorizationMapTest.php`:
1. Every route is classified: PUBLIC (listed with a reason), operator (must map to a permission in `RoutePermissions`), or signed-in; a signed-in route with an id must name its owner (passenger, driver, fleet owner, business admin, own row, token, team). A new route that is none of these fails.
2. A guest gets 401/302-to-login/403/404 from every non-public route.
3. An attacker who is simultaneously a passenger, a verified driver in another fleet, a fleet owner and a business admin calls every owned route with the victim's real ids (ride, active ride, offer, receipt, payment method, payout, payout account, earning, incident, support case and message, trip share, trusted contact, check-in, rating, verification and legacy documents, vehicle, driver profile, applicant profile, fleet, fleet manager, suspension, notification, saved place, business approval, department, policy, statement, member, invitation, team, team invitation). Reads must be 403/404; writes must not succeed (no 2xx, no 5xx). For reads, the owner gets 200 with the same URL, so the ids are meaningful. Nested ids are exercised in the same run (a fleet's vehicle with another fleet's profile, a business's approval or statement from another account, an offer of another driver). A mutation check (removing `Gate::authorize` from `RideController::show`) made the test fail, then was reverted.

Operator permission map: every `/ops`, `/money/ops`, `/safety/ops`, `/support/ops`, `/operator`, `/api/ehail/ops`, `/ops/pricing` route maps to a permission (no admin-only fallthrough); `ops.home` and `ops.notifications*` are open to any operator by design.

## 4. Web security (scope 4)

- CSP (enforced, D-1414): `default-src 'self'; script-src 'self' 'nonce-…' [+ 'unsafe-eval' on Livewire pages]; style-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline'; font-src 'self' data:; img-src 'self' data: blob: <tile host>; connect-src 'self' [+ Reverb socket]; worker-src 'self'; manifest-src 'self'; media-src 'self'; frame-src 'none'; frame-ancestors 'self'; object-src 'none'; base-uri 'self'; form-action 'self' https://checkout.paystack.com <InfoDot>; report-uri /csp-report`. Escape hatch: SECURITY_CSP_REPORT_ONLY=true.
- Render sweep in a real browser (Chromium pane, http://127.0.0.1:8620), with console filtered for CSP and the server's `/csp-report` log: as an operator/fleet owner/business admin/passenger (dev user) every page of the landing, legal, fare calculator, profile and teams, legacy dashboard/rides/ride/driver/fleet pages, passenger app (booking, trips, a completed and a live trip with the map, safety, help and a case, account, notifications), the whole ops centre (live board and map, rides incl. a live ride, drivers, verification incl. a document, safety incl. an incident, check-ins, patterns, reports, access log, support incl. a case, tariffs, simulator, promotions, catalogue, commission, service areas map, finance, payouts, reconciliation, ledger, insights, audit, team, fleets, business incl. an account, notifications), business portal (all pages incl. book with map), fleet portal (all pages incl. a driver and a vehicle), geo demo; as a guest the trip-share page and auth pages; as a driver every `/drive` page incl. two trips, plus legacy driver pages. Result: **zero CSP violations** (no console report, no `csp.violation` log line). Map tiles render; delegated row links work (clicked through). The only console errors were the dev Reverb socket refusing (no server running) and the pane's service-worker limitation.
- Permissions-Policy: `geolocation=(self)` only where the route has AllowGeolocation (passenger booking, trip, safety; driver app; geo demo); `camera=()`, `microphone=()`, `payment=()`, `usb=()` everywhere.
- Headers: HSTS (180 days, https only), X-Frame-Options SAMEORIGIN, nosniff, strict-origin-when-cross-origin, Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy same-origin; private files send their own sandboxing CSP.
- CSRF: every state-changing web route goes through `web` with CSRF, except the signed Paystack webhook and `/csp-report` (no session started). Session: http_only, same_site lax, Secure on https, AuthenticateSession (password change ends other sessions), 2FA available (Fortify).
- Rate limits in place: login (3 keys), 2FA, ride request, ride actions, feeds, heartbeat, location ingest (1 per 2 s), geocoder and routing proxies, quotes and estimates, SOS, safety writes (reports, support messages, shares), uploads, ratings, contacts, trip share (per IP; tokens are 256-bit), contact links, comms, money pay/cash/card setup (+ abuse limits), data export.
- Webhook: HMAC-SHA512 of the raw body with `hash_equals` before parsing, stored once per (provider, event key), every state change re-verified with Paystack's API, 256 KB bound.

## 5. Reviewed and sound

- Idempotent ride requests: per user, scope and key under a unique constraint, body hash compared, 409 while in progress, released on 5xx.
- One active ride per passenger and per driver: unique `active_passenger_id` / `active_driver_id`.
- Quotes: server-issued, owner, purpose and expiry checked, HMAC-SHA256 with `hash_equals`, consumed once under a row lock; no client amount accepted anywhere.
- Promotions: row lock, per-user count under the lock, conditional global/budget update, unique (promotion, ride).
- Offer acceptance: ride row lock, conditional update scoped to driver and expiry; board claims through the same state machine.
- Trip points: only the authenticated driver's own active ride; accuracy, future, stale and out-of-order filters; speed and jitter screening in DistanceMeter.
- Charging: one live attempt per ride and purpose under the ride lock, unique idempotency key and reference, final rows never change, paid only after verify matches reference, amount and currency; card-setup references can never mark a ride paid.
- Refunds: finance.refund, row lock, never above refundable minus open refunds, audited, processed only on Paystack's confirmation.
- Ledger: `Ledger::post` refuses unbalanced journals; PostgreSQL deferred balance trigger, immutability triggers and one-sided CHECK; SQLite immutability and one-sided triggers; model guards on update/delete.
- Incentive awards: row locks, unique source key, budget check. Business limits: atomic conditional reservations; a ride cannot be moved onto a business account after booking.
- Live location: a passenger sees only the assigned driver while tracking; drivers never receive a passenger position; ops live feed needs drivers.positions, `no-store`.
- Trip share: 256-bit token (only its hash stored), same 404 for unknown/revoked/expired, `noindex`, `no-store`, `no-referrer`, no session; first names, plate and a 3-decimal position.
- Identity documents (R-46 path): private disk, sniffed and re-encoded, streamed through `PrivateFileResponse` after policy + role narrowing, every view audited; identity and licence numbers encrypted with HMAC blind indexes; fleet portal shows masked numbers only with a grant.
- Encryption at rest: driver identifiers, payout account holder and number, Paystack authorisation code, trusted contacts, push endpoints and keys, SMS numbers, business rider phone, trip PIN.
- Logs: ids and exception classes only; Paystack webhook payloads stored with customer, authorisation, recipient, metadata and IP removed; CSP reports keep directive and host only.
- Secrets: none in built assets (only the public Reverb app key, by design); `.env` not tracked.
- Broadcasting: one private channel `ride.{id}`, passenger or driver only, fail-closed.
